# Plan Review A — a peak-RSS baseline for the plain trimming path in CI

**Reviewer:** A (independent)
**Plan:** `plans/08192026_plain-path-memory-ci/PLAN.md`
**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Verified against:** `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (job `clumpify-memory`, lines 271–453),
`.github/clumpify-memory-baseline.tsv`, `examples/mk_clump_fixture.rs`, `src/main.rs`,
`src/parallel.rs`, `src/clump.rs`, `src/adapter.rs`, `src/fastq.rs`, commits `2ab0918` and `1595d59`.

## Verdict

The gap is real and the design is the right shape. Three findings are load-bearing enough to change the
plan before implementation:

1. **The regression side of the banding inequality is quoted from the wrong tree.** The plan's binding
   constraint (`width <= 18 MiB - margin`) is the delta `1595d59` would have had under the *platform*
   allocator. The tree is mimalloc, where the same commit moved plain `c4` by **−6.4 MiB**, and that
   figure is independently corroborated by the commit's own message (`plain -c4 97 -> 91 MiB`,
   macOS/arm64). No width can satisfy both sides against a 6 MiB target on a GitHub runner.
2. **The RSS response to a reservation change is allocator-mediated and changes sign.** In the lead's own
   2x2 the reserved bytes *fell* at 65 bp, yet stock RSS *rose* 17.6 MiB and mimalloc RSS fell 6.4 MiB.
   So the right-hand side of the rule cannot be transferred from oxy at all — it has to be measured on
   `ubuntu-latest`, in KiB, by a deliberate revert. The plan has no step that produces that number.
3. **`plain_c1` cannot satisfy the plan's own positive-engagement assertion.** `src/main.rs:858` guards
   the banner with `if cli.cores > 1`, so a `--cores 1` run prints no `Using N worker threads` line at
   all. As written, Behavior 5 fails that cell unconditionally, and there is no positive marker for the
   serial path in the log.

Everything else is refinement. The plan's statistical instincts are sound — refusing to band from 3
oxy reps, arming the lower bound, and questioning saturation are all correct calls, and (b)/(c) in its
own self-review are the two best decisions in the document.

## What checks out

Verified rather than taken on trust:

- The six idioms the plan says it will reuse all exist in `clumpify-memory` as described: the
  capture-then-probe GNU-time check with an apt fallback (lines 296–318), the 8 GiB assertion
  (`8388608` KiB, line 324), capture-status-parse in `measure()` (lines 379–391), the `^clumpify:`
  engagement grep (line 386), the `# BOOTSTRAP` suspend (lines 351–356), and the empty-field `case`
  guard plus KiB-side comparison (lines 396–408).
- **The gzip claim is correct.** `src/main.rs:438` sets `gzip = naming::output_is_gzipped(cli.dont_gzip,
  &cli.input[0])` — output encoding follows `input[0]` and `--gzip` does not force it. The
  `ls "$out"/*.fq.gz` assertion is well-founded, and PE naming (`*_val_{1,2}.fq.gz`) matches the glob.
- **`--cores 1` really is a different path**, not just a narrower one: `src/main.rs:1614` dispatches to
  `FastqReader::open` (no threaded decompressor, no worker pool) when `cores == 1 && !clumpify`.
- **No core-count clamp exists** (`available_parallelism` appears nowhere in `src/`), so `--cores 4` is
  honoured verbatim on a 4-vCPU runner.
- **The plain path prints no prediction.** Confirmed: the banner's predicted peak comes from
  `ClumpLayout`, and `--memory` is only consumed through `resolve_clump_layout`. The plan's core
  structural premise holds.
- **Runtime is a non-issue.** Three recent `clumpify-memory` runs took 5m12s, 5m45s and 5m29s (run ids
  32156634406, 32157208637, 32179108913) for seven cells plus fixture generation. The 150 bp fixture
  roughly triples generated bytes, so budget ~10–12 min against the proposed 30. The plan's efficiency

- **10M pairs is not needed for saturation, and the peak is input-size-independent.** `src/adapter.rs:135`
  scans streaming with no retention; `src/parallel.rs` holds `BATCH_SIZE = 4096` records across
  `channel_depths(false) = {work: 2, result_per_core: 2}` plus one in hand — bounded by `cores`, not by
  input. Steady occupancy needs roughly `cores x 5 x 4096` ~ 82k pairs at c4. See the caveat in §3.4
  before shrinking the fixture, though: it is not a free win.

## 1. The threshold arithmetic (Validation 7)

### 1.1 The events, separated

`2ab0918` is an ancestor of `1595d59` (verified), and `2ab0918` landed while the flat reservation was
still in the tree. So each commit's own delta is a single row of the 2x2, never the diagonal:

| event | cell | before | after | delta | relative |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `2ab0918` (mimalloc) | c4 | 59.2 | 104.0 | **+44.8** | +75.7% |
| `2ab0918` (mimalloc) | c8 | 74.0 | 161.0 | **+87.0** | +117.6% (2.18x) |
| `1595d59` (reservation) | c4 | 104.0 | 97.6 | **−6.4** | −6.2% |
| `1595d59` (reservation) | c8 | 161.0 | 146.0 | **−15.0** | −9.3% |

Against that table, three numbers in the plan need correcting:

- **Goal, "`2ab0918` raised peak ~1.9x at `--cores 8` — 74 → 146 MiB".** 74.0 is stock/flat and 146.0 is
  mimalloc/estimator: this is the diagonal, i.e. both commits combined. `2ab0918`'s own move was
  74.0 → 161.0, **2.18x**, and `1595d59` then took 161 → 146. The plan understates `2ab0918` and
  silently credits it with `1595d59`'s improvement.
- **Implementation step 6, "mimalloc at ~+72 MiB (c8)".** Same diagonal. The single-commit figure is
  +87.0 MiB — and it belongs to a cell that will not exist in CI, since a 4-vCPU runner has no c8 cell.
- **Goal, "`1595d59` ... cost ~18 MiB at `--cores 4`".** That is the stock-allocator counterfactual
  (59.2 → 76.8). `1595d59` landed on a mimalloc tree, where it was an **improvement at every measured
  cell** (−6.4 at c4, −15.0 at c8). Presented as history, this is a regression that never occurred on
  any tree that existed. The lesson the plan draws from it — that improvements invalidate a baseline too
  — is the right one and does not need the counterfactual to stand up.

This matters beyond tidiness: these figures are destined for the baseline file's header as its evidence
base, and this repo has a standing pattern of corrective copy carrying the defect it corrects.

### 1.2 Is a single width per cell viable for both events?

The question as the plan poses it is malformed, and answering it properly is what exposes finding 1.
Per cell there is exactly one delta per event, and the two events sit at different core counts, one of
which is not a CI cell. Reframed per cell, on the tree we actually have:

- **Allocator-class change at `plain_c4`:** +43% to +76% depending on which side you normalise. Any
  width below ~40% catches it.
- **Reservation-class change at `plain_c4`:** −6.2%. Corroborated on a second host and a different
  fixture by `1595d59`'s own commit message (97 → 91 MiB, −6.2% — the two hosts agree to a tenth of a
  point). Only a width under ~6% catches it.

So there is no single width, and step 6's "the narrower of these is what the band must still catch"
drives the width toward a target it cannot reach. Which brings us to whether the small target is
reachable at all.

### 1.3 Can the banding rule be satisfied on a GitHub-hosted runner?

It can be answered now, from data already in the repo, rather than deferred to the bootstrap. The
clumpify job's ratio denominator is deterministic — `pred = budget x 10/11`, and the job asserts exactly
that within 2048 KiB (lines 404–408) — so `pred = 930.9 MiB` for every 1G cell and the recorded ratio
ranges convert **exactly** into absolute-MiB spreads observed on `ubuntu-latest`:

| cell | n | mean ratio | range | mean peak MiB | abs range MiB | half-range MiB | half-range / mean | min width (3x) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `1G_c4` | 27 | 0.7883 | 0.055 | 733.8 | 51.2 | 25.6 | **3.49%** | 10.5% |
| `25bp_1G_c4` | 27 | 0.9138 | 0.043 | 850.5 | 40.0 | 20.0 | 2.35% | 7.1% |
| `1G_c2` | 25 | 0.8789 | 0.034 | 818.2 | 31.6 | 15.8 | 1.93% | 5.8% |
| `multipair_c4` | 27 | 0.7987 | 0.081 | 743.5 | 75.4 | 37.7 | 5.07% | 15.2% |
| `1G_c4_fastqc` | 27 | 0.7069 | 0.080 | 658.0 | 74.5 | 37.2 | 5.66% | 17.0% |

Two conclusions.

**A plausible width exists — for the class of change that actually broke.** A width of 15–25% of the
mean is 4x–7x the clumpify-derived half-range and still well under the 43–76% allocator move. The gate
can arm. The plan is not committing to something impossible, and it should say so up front instead of
leaving it as a "remaining risk".

**The reservation class is out of reach, for two independent reasons.** The 3x rule alone puts the floor
at 5.8–17% against a 6.2% target, and the transfer argument in §1.4 says the target may not even have
that sign in CI. Anything the plan says about catching an 18 MiB reservation move should be struck.

One caution on transferring the table: the clumpify peak is dominated by a large, always-resident bin
pool that *dilutes* the scheduling-jitter term. On the plain path the jitter term **is** the peak —
`cores x 5` batch slots at ~3 MB each accounts for essentially all of the observed 59–104 MiB at c4 — so
±1–2 slots of occupancy is ±3–6% of the reading. Expect plain-cell relative noise to be **at least** as
wide as `1G_c4`'s 3.49%, plausibly 4–6%. Treat 10.5% as the optimistic floor for `plain_c4`.

### 1.4 Why the regression side has to be measured in CI

The reserve is sized from *uncompressed* record bytes but holds *compressed* output, so it is a large
over-reserve, and how much of it becomes resident is entirely the allocator's decision. The evidence
that this is not a small effect is in the lead's own 2x2, read against the code:

`estimated_record_bytes` is `id + 1 + seq + 1 + 2 + qual + 1` (`src/clump.rs:355`); with this generator's
~41-byte header the crossover against the old flat 300 B/record is at **L = 127 bp** — matching
`1595d59`'s "above roughly 125 bp the reserve grows rather than shrinks". At the oxy fixture's 65 bp the
reserve therefore **fell** by ~0.5 MB per buffer (~20 MB across 40 resident buffers at c4) — and yet
stock RSS **rose** 17.6 MiB while mimalloc RSS fell only 6.4 MiB. The response is opposite in sign to
the change under one allocator and a third of its magnitude under the other.

Two consequences the plan should absorb:

- **The RHS cannot be imported.** Not from another allocator, not from another host, and not reliably in
  sign. The clumpify baseline earned its RHS in the same units on the same host ("lever A fires by
  0.036"); this plan needs the equivalent. Concretely: before the banding PR, run the job twice on a
  scratch branch — once as-is, once with `src/main.rs:29-30`'s `#[global_allocator]` reverted to the
  platform allocator — and record the per-cell delta in KiB. That is the number that goes on the RHS.
- **The 51 bp and 150 bp cells respond in opposite directions** to a reservation change (0.49x flat at
  51 bp, 1.15x at 150 bp). That is a much better reason to keep both cells than "the read-length axis",
  and it means Validation 7 must be a per-cell table with signs, not one inequality.

## 2. The bootstrap, and whether it converges

The path from BOOTSTRAP to a band is the least specified part of the plan, and it has a structural
problem the clumpify precedent did not.

**Pooling readings across `dev` is invalid here.** The clumpify baseline's closing paragraph says to
judge whether a change can reach the clump path before pooling new observations with the old — and for
that job most `src/` commits cannot. For the plain path essentially every `src/` and `Cargo.lock` change
can reach it. Passive accumulation over dev history therefore mixes draws from trees with different true
means, which inflates the half-range with exactly the signal the band exists to detect, and biases the
width upward — permanently, since a too-wide band never fires and so never gets revisited.

**Fix, and it also answers "when do we stop".** Gather the whole sample on **one pinned commit** by
re-running the job: `gh run rerun` allocates a fresh runner and yields an independent draw, and a run is
~5.5 min (measured). Fifteen to twenty draws is an afternoon, or less in parallel. Note that `ci.yml` has
**no `workflow_dispatch` trigger** (only `push`, `pull_request`, `schedule`), so add one or the rerun
route is the only option. Preregister n, then decide once — the sample becomes a deliberate experiment
rather than a passive accumulation, and "who decides / when" stops being open.

**The stop rule the plan is missing**, stated so the banding PR is mechanical:

1. n >= 15 draws, all from one commit SHA, recorded in the file.
2. If `3 x half-range <= target delta - margin`, band at the chosen width.
3. Otherwise record with the reason and the numbers, exactly as the clumpify file does for
   `multipair_c4` and `1G_c4_fastqc`. This is a valid outcome, not a failure — say so in the plan so
   nobody re-opens the question every quarter.
4. Record n, the range, the multiple of half-range, and the sample's commit SHA in the file. The
   clumpify file records n and log count; the SHA is the new requirement that follows from the
   homogeneity problem above.

**On the half-range estimator itself.** Range grows with n by construction: for a normal, `E[range]` is
~3.08 sigma at n=10 and ~3.9 sigma at n=27. A width of `3 x half-range` from n=10 is therefore materially
tighter in sigma terms than the same rule applied at n=27, which is where the clumpify cells were banded.
Since draws here cost 5 minutes, prefer n >= 20 and treat "double-digit" as the floor it is, not the
target.

**If it never converges**, the plan's fallback (record, don't band) is right, but it should name which
cells are *expected* to land there so the outcome reads as predicted rather than as a stalled plan. On
the table in §1.3 my prediction is: `plain_c4` and `plain_c4_150bp` band at 15–25%; `plain_c2` probably
bands; `plain_c4_fastqc` does not (as the plan already says); `plain_c1` should not be banded on a
percentage basis at all (§3.5).

## 3. Logic, assumptions, edge cases

### 3.1 `plain_c1` breaks the banner assertion (finding 3)

`src/main.rs:858` — `if cli.cores > 1 { eprintln!("Using {} worker threads ...") }`. A `--cores 1` run
prints nothing, so a `grep -q "Using 1 worker threads"` fails the cell every time. Worse, once the check
is dropped for that cell, `plain_c1` has **no** positive-engagement assertion — the exact hole the
`^clumpify:` grep exists to close, reopened for one cell.

Best available substitute, and it is worth applying to *every* cell rather than just c1: assert the
record count from the trimming report,
`grep -q 'Total reads processed: *10,000,000' "$out"/*_trimming_report.txt` (`src/report.rs:337`,
`format_number` inserts the commas). That proves the whole fixture was consumed. The plan currently has
no such check, so a short read — a partially generated fixture, a truncated write — produces a
*lower* peak that during bootstrap silently pollutes the sample rather than failing. For c1, pair it
with an assertion that the banner is **absent**, which at least catches an accidental `cores > 1`.

### 3.2 Free space is asserted once, before the largest writes

The plan inherits the 8 GiB pre-flight and its rationale — a stalled writer inflates peak RSS — but this
job generates roughly 3x the model job's fixture bytes (~900 MB for the 150 bp pair against ~340 MB at
51 bp) and each 150 bp cell writes ~1 GB of output at the default gzip level 1
(`src/fastq.rs:29`, `DEFAULT_GZIP_LEVEL = 1`, "~75% larger" files). Headroom on `ubuntu-latest` is real,
so I do not predict a failure — but the guard is cheap and the failure mode is a *fabricated high*
reading that looks like data, which this project has already been burned by once. Re-assert free space
after generation and between cells, and keep the model job's `rm -f "$out"/*.fq.gz` (the plan never
mentions it).

### 3.3 Baseline-invalidating knobs are not enumerated

The plan names the allocator. The baseline also moves with `BATCH_SIZE` (`src/parallel.rs:33`),
`channel_depths(false)` (`src/clump.rs:193-198`), the two `with_capacity` reservations in
`process_batch` (`src/parallel.rs:353-354`, `1080`), `DEFAULT_GZIP_LEVEL`, `Cargo.lock`, the fixture
size and read length, and **the order the cells run in** (the first cell reads a cold page cache; a
reader that blocks lowers occupancy and lowers the peak — a stable bias, not noise, but one that moves
if the order changes). List these in the header. This is the plain-path analogue of the clumpify file's
`git diff --quiet ... -- src Cargo.lock` note, and it is more necessary here, not less.

Also decide `--compression` explicitly: the cells inherit level 1 today, so a future default change
silently re-baselines the job. Either pass `--compression 1` (immune, but no longer tracks the shipped
default) or leave it and list it as a re-baseline trigger. I lean to the latter — tracking the shipped
path is the point — but it should be a decision in the file, not an omission.

### 3.4 Validation 6 can misdiagnose max-statistics as non-saturation

Saturation is better established than the plan thinks (§"What checks out"), but the test as written can
still produce a wrong answer in the *other* direction. Peak RSS is a maximum over the run, so `E[peak]`
rises with run length independently of any buffer growing: a 20M-pair run has twice as many chances to
hit worst-case occupancy as a 10M-pair one. Validation 6 will therefore likely show a small systematic
increase, and the plan's stated response — "the fixture must grow before banding" — chases that forever.

Restate it as a one-off size sweep (1M / 2M / 5M / 10M / 20M in one job) and look for the knee, with
acceptance defined as "the step between the top two sizes is smaller than the half-range" rather than
"within noise".

The same effect is a useful lever, and it cuts against shrinking the fixture for speed: because maxima
concentrate near the ceiling as the number of opportunities grows, a **longer** run gives a higher and
*tighter* distribution. If the bootstrap shows the half-range too wide to band, the first thing to try
is a bigger fixture, not more reps.

### 3.5 `plain_c1` is a poor percentage-gate candidate

At ~9 MiB the reading is dominated by static footprint — the binary, mimalloc's arenas, statics. A 3 MiB
change from an unrelated dependency is 33% there, so a percentage band will fire for benign reasons.
Keep the cell (the serial path deserves a tripwire) but band it, if at all, in absolute KiB with a
generous width, as a "did the serial path explode" check rather than a drift gate.

### 3.6 Smaller points

- **Symmetric or asymmetric width?** The rule quoted from the baseline file describes one width, but the
  clumpify job gates asymmetrically (+0.09 / −0.10). Behavior 7 should say which, and if symmetric, why
  the precedent is not followed.
- **A stack of sub-threshold moves passes.** With a 15–25% width, three 6% regressions land unnoticed
  and are then absorbed by the next re-baseline. The mitigation is trend-watching, not a tighter band:
  print each reading in a fixed machine-parseable form (`PLAIN_PEAK label=... kib=...`) and record the
  running summary in the file's comments, because Actions log retention (90 days) bounds how far back a
  trend can be reconstructed from logs alone.
- **State the expected value.** From the mimalloc/estimator row, the per-worker slope is
  `(146.0 - 97.6)/4 = 12.1 MiB/worker` with a 49.2 MiB intercept; scaling the per-worker term by record
  bytes (148/176 at 51 bp vs 65 bp) predicts `plain_c4` at **~90 MiB**. Put that in the plan so the very
  first CI reading can be sanity-checked instead of accepted. A reading of ~30 MiB would mean the cell
  never reaches steady occupancy, and nothing in the plan as written would notice.

## 4. Alternatives on the normalisation question

The plan is right that no platform-independent gate is available without a printed prediction, and an
absolute-KiB baseline on a shared runner is viable — the §1.3 table shows 2–6% relative dispersion on
this very host, which is workable for a 15–25% width. Three alternatives, assessed rather than listed:

**Per-worker slope instead of absolute peak.** Fit peak across the c1/c2/c4 cells the plan already
proposes; band the slope and the intercept separately. Attraction: it isolates precisely the term both
events moved, and it is host-independent enough to run on macOS too. Under mimalloc the slope is
12.1 MiB/worker; under the stock allocator it is **0.025** (76.8 at c4 vs 76.9 at c8 — flat), so
`2ab0918` would have shown up as `0 -> 12` MiB/worker, which is about as loud as a signal gets. Against
it: a difference of two noisy readings has larger absolute dispersion (~1.4x a single cell's) on a
smaller base, so ~5% relative — no better than the absolute figure. **Recommendation:** derive and print
it during bootstrap (free — the cells already exist), decide with data, don't commit now.

**Ratio to another cell (e.g. `c4/c1`).** Cancels common-mode runner noise and is platform-independent.
But it is blind to any change that scales both cells — which is the same failure the clumpify baseline
documents for gating on the promise alone ("a work-channel-depth revert ... every cell it moved still
sat under the printed peak"). Useful as a recorded diagnostic, not as the gate.

**Ratio to input size.** Reject. Peak is input-size-independent by construction here, so normalising by
input size only injects the fixture size as a hidden factor.

**The thing that would actually solve it** is out of scope under the plan's "no source change" fix, but
worth naming for a follow-up: the plain path's peak is *predictable* from
`cores x slots x BATCH_SIZE x record_bytes x 2 mates`. Printing that figure at startup would give the
plain path the same absolute, platform-independent promise gate the clumpify path has, and would make
this job's band a secondary check rather than the only one. That is a much stronger long-term answer
than any normalisation of the measurement.
